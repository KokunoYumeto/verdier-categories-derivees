\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-3}
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.3.3.}\quad
Let $C$ be an \emph{additive} category. To every object $X$ of $C$, associate
the complex in $C$ whose components are all zero except for the degree-zero
component, which is equal to $X$. This defines a functor from $C$ to
$\operatorname{comp}(C)$ and, by composing with the canonical functor
$\operatorname{comp}(C)\longrightarrow K(C)$, a functor from $C$ to $K(C)$.

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-3-full-embeddings}
These functors are fully faithful and injective on objects, and thus realize
$C$ as a full subcategory of $\operatorname{comp}(C)$ and $K(C)$. Henceforth,
the category $C$ will always be regarded as a full subcategory of
$\operatorname{comp}(C)$ and $K(C)$ through these functors.

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-4}
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.3.4.}\quad
Let $C$ be an \emph{abelian} category. For every complex $X$ in $C$, denote
by $H^0(X)$ the object of $C$

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-4-1}
\[
  H^0(X)=\operatorname{Ker}(d_X^0)/\operatorname{Im}(d_X^{-1}).
  \tag{1.3.4.1}
\]

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-4-induced-morphism}
For every morphism of complexes $X\xrightarrow{f}Y$, denote by
\[
  H^0(f)\colon H^0(X)\longrightarrow H^0(Y)
\]
\noindent the morphism induced by $f$. This defines a functor

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-4-comp-functor}
\[
  H^0\colon\operatorname{comp}(C)\longrightarrow C
\]
\noindent called the \emph{zeroth cohomology object}. This functor sends
homotopic morphisms to equal morphisms and consequently yields a functor,
again denoted

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch2-1-3-4-k-functor}
\[
  H^0\colon K(C)\longrightarrow C.
\]
