\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-5}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Lemma 3.3.5.}\quad
\textit{Let $m\colon W\longrightarrow W'$, $m'\colon W'\longrightarrow W''$,
$m''=m'm$ be three morphisms of $\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}}$ belonging
to $\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}})$. The morphisms
$F(m)$, $F(m')$, and $F(m'')$ were defined above in cases 1), 2), 3), 4),
5), 6), and one has:}

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-5-1}
\begin{equation}
F(m'')=F(m')F(m).
\tag{3.3.5.1}
\end{equation}

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-6}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3.3.6.}\quad
(Definition of the category $\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}}$ by generators
and relations). \textit{Let $\mathcal C$ be a category and let}

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-6-object-map}
\[
\operatorname{Ob}(F)\colon
\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}})
\longrightarrow \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal C),
\]

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-6-arrow-map}
\[
\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(F)\colon
\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}})
\longrightarrow \mathcal F\ell(\mathcal C)
\]
\textit{be two maps compatible with the source and target maps of the
categories $\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}}$ and $\mathcal C$, such that for
every triple of morphisms $m\colon W\longrightarrow W'$,
$m'\colon W'\longrightarrow W''$, $m''=m'm$,
$m,m',m''\in\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}})$,
one has:}

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-6-relation}
\[
\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(F)(m')\,
\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(F)(m)
=\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(F)(m'').
\]

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-6-extension}
\textit{Then there exists a functor
$F\colon\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}}\longrightarrow\mathcal C$, and only
one, such that $F$ induces the map $\operatorname{Ob}(F)$ on the sets of objects and such that,
for every $m\in\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Ext}})$,
one has:}
\[
F(m)=\mathcal F\ell_{\leq 1}(F)(m).
\]

Indeed, one easily checks that the connected sequence of functors associated with
the functor supplied by (3.3.5) and (3.3.6) is precisely the sequence
$(F^n,\delta_F^n)$ with which we started.

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch3d-3-3-7}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.3.7. Proof of Lemma 3.3.5.}\quad
The proof proceeds by examining the different possible cases. First, there
are four cases to examine when at least two of the objects $W$, $W'$, $W''$
are zero. The morphisms $F(m)$, $F(m')$, and $F(m'')$ are then defined in
one of cases 1), 2), or 3). The verification of (3.3.5.1) is immediate and
is left to the reader. Next, there are nine cases to examine in which one of
the objects $W$, $W'$, $W''$ is zero. Two of the morphisms $m$, $m'$, $m''$
are then defined by 1), 2), 3), the third being defined by 4), 5), 6). Here
again the verification is immediate and is left to the reader. It remains
to examine the cases where $W$, $W'$, $W''$ are nonzero. There are eight
such cases:

\noindent$c_1)$ $m$ has degree less than 0, and $m'<0$ means that the latter morphism also has degree less than 0;

\noindent$c_2)$ $m<0$ means that the former morphism has degree less than 0, and $m'=0$ means that the latter has degree 0;
