\noindent\textbf{1.4.2.}\quad
Let $e\to K\xrightarrow{n}H\xrightarrow{f}G\to e$ be an exact
sequence of groups, with $K$ identified by $n$ with a normal subgroup
of $H$, and let $(\mathcal C,G,T,c)$ and
$(\mathcal C',G,T',c')$ be two $G$-categories. Let
$(\mathcal C,H,T_f,c_f)$ and $(\mathcal C',H,T'_f,c'_f)$ be the
$H$-categories obtained by change of groups along
$H\xrightarrow{f}G$. Finally, let

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-2-eq1}
\begin{equation}
  (F,m)\colon(\mathcal C,H,T_f,c_f)
  \longrightarrow(\mathcal C',H,T'_f,c'_f)
  \tag{1.4.2.1}
\end{equation}
be an $H$-functor. The diagrams (1.2.3.2) then give, for every pair of
elements $k,k'\in K$, a commutative diagram:

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-2-eq2}
\begin{center}
  $\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&,column sep=5em,row sep=3.5em]
    F \arrow[rr,"{m(kk')}"] \arrow[dr,"{m(k)}"']
      \& \& F \\
      \& F \arrow[ur,"{m(k')}"'] \&
  \end{tikzcd}$,
  \hfill\textup{(1.4.2.2)}
\end{center}

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-2-representation}
and hence a representation of $K^\circ$ in the group of automorphisms
of $F$. When the functor $(F,m)$ comes from a $G$-functor, this
representation is trivial. Conversely, when this representation is
trivial, it follows immediately from the diagrams (1.2.3.2) that, for
every $h\in H$ and every $k\in K$, one has

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-2-eq3}
\begin{equation}
  m(hk)=m(kh)=m(h)
  \tag{1.4.2.3}
\end{equation}
and therefore the $H$-functor $(F,m)$ comes from a $G$-functor by the
change of group $f^*$. In the remainder of this paragraph we propose to
study, in a very special case, the $H$-functors $(F,m)$ that induce a
preassigned representation of $K^\circ$ in the group of automorphisms
of $F$.

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-3}
\noindent\textbf{1.4.3.}\quad
We use the notation of (1.4.2). Suppose that the category $\mathcal C'$
is additive and that $K=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The group of signs
$\{-1,+1\}\simeq\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ then acts on the identity
functor of the category $\mathcal C'$ by associating to every object
$X$ of $\mathcal C'$ the morphism

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-3-eq1}
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon(X)\colon
  X\xrightarrow{\,\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{id}_X\,}X,
  \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}.
  \tag{1.4.3.1}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the group of signs acts on every functor
$F\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal C'$ by associating to every object $Y$
of $\mathcal C$ the morphism

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-4-3-eq2}
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon(Y)\colon
  F(Y)\xrightarrow{\,\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{id}_{F(Y)}\,}F(Y),
  \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}.
  \tag{1.4.3.2}
\end{equation}
