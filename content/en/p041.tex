\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-3-6-remark}
It should be noted that, even when $G^\circ$ acts strictly on the
categories, the functors do not necessarily commute strictly with the
actions of $G^\circ$. Thus, when $G^\circ$ acts trivially on the
categories $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal C'$ (\textit{i.e.}
$T(g)=T'(g)=$ identity), a $G^\circ$-functor is then nothing other than
a functor equipped with a representation of $G$ in its automorphism
group. Moreover, it is easy to see that a $G^\circ$-functor is not
necessarily isomorphic to a strict $G^\circ$-functor. A counterexample
is easily constructed by taking inspiration from [19].

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-3-6-strictify}
Using the results of [\emph{loc. cit.}], one can moreover show that every
$G^\circ$-category is $G^\circ$-equivalent to a strict
$G^\circ$-category. By the same methods one can also sharpen this result
as follows. Given two $G^\circ$-categories
$(\mathcal C,G^\circ,T,c)$ and $(\mathcal C',G^\circ,T',c')$, there
exist two strict $G^\circ$-categories
$(\widetilde{\mathcal C},G^\circ,\widetilde T)$ and
$(\widetilde{\mathcal C}',G^\circ,\widetilde T')$ and
$G^\circ$-equivalences

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-3-6-equivalences}
\[
  (u,\mu)\colon
  (\widetilde{\mathcal C},G^\circ,\widetilde T)
  \longrightarrow
  (\mathcal C,G^\circ,T,c),
\]
\[
  (v,\nu)\colon
  (\mathcal C',G^\circ,T',c')
  \longrightarrow
  (\widetilde{\mathcal C}',G^\circ,\widetilde T')
\]
such that for every functor

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-3-6-functor}
\[
  (F,m)\colon
  (\mathcal C,G^\circ,T,c)
  \longrightarrow
  (\mathcal C',G^\circ,T',c'),
\]
the composite functor

\VerdierSemanticAnchor{ch1-1-3-6-composite}
\[
  (v,\nu)(F,m)(u,\mu)\colon
  (\widetilde{\mathcal C},G^\circ,\widetilde T)
  \longrightarrow
  (\widetilde{\mathcal C}',G^\circ,\widetilde T')
\]
is isomorphic to a functor that commutes strictly with the actions of
$G^\circ$. We shall not use this result.
